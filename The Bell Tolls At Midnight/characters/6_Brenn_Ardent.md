# Father Brenn Ardent
*Priest of the Eternal Flame, Former Mercenary, Shepherd of Gravedawn*

## Role in the Story
Brenn is Gravedawn's father—the priest who runs the chapel's daily life alongside brothers, acolytes, and the faithful who keep the pews from emptying entirely. He is the town's quiet backbone: he stands between institutional Church pressure and the lived needs of a dying harbor town. He climbs the cursed tower with the Guild party because he suspects foul play in how the witch was killed. If confirming that truth requires it, he will disclose Church secrets to the party. He prioritizes the town over the Church—but he will not act on that suspicion until he has seen the situation himself.

## Physical Description
- Age: Late 40s to early 50s
- Build: Lean, enduring; the body of a man who once fought for coin and still keeps the habits of field work
- Appearance: Weathered face; hands marked by old burn scars and older blade work
- Notable Features:
  - Scarred palms and fingers (pre-vow mercenary injuries)
  - Smells faintly of old books, candle wax, and rain on clean stone
  - Moves with the economy of someone who has packed a travel case many times
  - Voice: low, gravelly; dry humor that arrives late and leaves quickly

## Backstory

### Before the Cloth
Brenn was a mercenary. He fought and killed for coin with a company that took the jobs others would not. After a misfortune, the rest of his party died and he alone washed up in Gravedawn—a broken man with nowhere left to go. Harbin gave him shelter for a time, then eventually kicked him out. By then Brenn had come into contact with a few priests of the Church; he went to them for help, and in time joined the cloth and took the oath. He has always held Harbin as a friend: a man who helped a stranger when he was down, and who gave him space and time to face his grief.

### The Boy Behind the Tanner's Shed
Years ago, Brenn found a street boy beaten half to death behind the tanner's shed—Tovin, who had stolen to eat and paid for it in blood. Brenn took him in. While the boy's injuries healed, Brenn taught him to read. Tovin owned almost nothing: one book pulled from trash, kept for its drawings long before he could read the words. When he could finally read it, he gave the book to Brenn as payment for shelter, teaching, and the chance to become someone other than a thief with empty pockets.

Brenn accepted it the way he accepts cracked cups and three-legged toy horses from the poor: so the debt ends clean, without gratitude becoming a chain. He set preservation spells into the binding and pages and placed the book in the vestry display cupboard—not because it was rare, but because the boy who gave it had stopped being only a boy the day he placed it there.

### Present
Brenn keeps the chapel open, feeds dock families, steadies the frightened, and takes small tokens instead of coin so no one in Gravedawn owes the Church their dignity. He trusts Brother Tovin to hold the chapel's daily care if the hill does not return him. He suspects the Church's clean record of the tower will not survive honest light—and he knows Prelate Orsan will demand silence anyway. Brenn will keep that silence only until the tower itself confirms what he fears.

## Core Personality
- **Practical mercy:** Comfort first, then work. Prayer without bread is half a lie.
- **Dry, understated humor:** Uses it to blunt fear, rarely to entertain.
- **Protective without smothering:** Names people into responsibility rather than shielding them from it.
- **Loyal to the town before the institution:** The Flame matters; the living who kneel under it matter more.
- **Quietly haunted:** Remembers dead company members; does not recite their names lightly.
- **Deliberate with secrets:** Suspicion is not proof. He will not burn the Church's reputation on rumor alone—but he will burn it for the town if the hill proves him right.

## Motivations
- Keep Gravedawn's people fed, blessed, and as unafraid as honesty allows.
- Climb the tower, confirm the foul play he suspects, and break what he can of the curse before Solstice closes the window.
- Disclose Church secrets to the Guild party if the town's survival requires it—and only then.
- Leave Tovin standing if Brenn himself does not come back.

## Fears
- Being wrong about the witch's death—and being right too late.
- Becoming the kind of churchman who buries truth to protect reputation.
- Watching another party die while he still holds the piece that might have saved them.
- That care itself is not enough—and never was.

## Mannerisms & Body Language
- Traces grooves in windowsills, prayer-book edges, cracked cups—habit of hands that need work.
- Touches a shoulder not quite as blessing, not quite as farewell: naming someone into place.
- Sets gloves, cloak, and case with deliberate order before hard conversations finish.
- Looks at the hill even when the window cannot show it.

## Talking Style (for dialogue)
- Short sentences. Lets silence do part of the work.
- Corrects gently: "Both can be true." / "Good. One of me has been enough trouble."
- Rarely explains his past; refers to shared history in fragments only the other person would catch.
- Does not promise what he cannot keep. Especially return.

### Example Voice Lines
- "Harbin asked for help. Help came."
- "Standing in unfortunate places is most of the work."
- "No matter what happens on that hill, this town will still have a capable priest."
- "Keep the Flame lit."

## Relationship: Brother Tovin
Brenn raised Tovin from street survival into literacy, faith, and chapel work. He does not treat him as a child anymore—and that is the point of leaving him the ledger, the lamps, and the town. Their shared history sits under conversations as touchstones (the book, the cupboard tokens, morning bread routes), not as stories retold. Brenn trusts Tovin's competence more than Tovin trusts it himself.

## Relationship: Harbin
Harbin sheltered Brenn when he washed up broken after his company died, then eventually put him out—an act Brenn still counts among the better things done for him. The townmaster gave a stranger room to face grief without sermon. Brenn has held him as a friend ever since. Their bond is old, practical, and largely unspoken: two men who keep Gravedawn upright from different desks.

## Values and Red Lines
- No debt-chains on the poor; take a token, close the matter.
- Town before Church when the two cannot both be kept.
- No burning institutional secrets on suspicion alone—confirm first, then act.
- No abandoning the town's daily care for institutional theater.

## Abilities and Expertise
- Field exorcism under pressure; recognition of shifting anchors mid-rite
- Pastoral care: rites, infirmary roster, community logistics
- Preservation and minor protective magics (as used on the cupboard book)
- Pre-vow mercenary combat experience; can follow adventuring command when required
- Intimate knowledge of Gravedawn's people, paths, and the tower's official (and unofficial) history
