# Brother Tovin
*Acolyte of the Eternal Flame, Keeper of Gravedawn's Daily Care*

## Role in the Story
Tovin is Father Brenn's junior and the man left holding the chapel when Brenn climbs the tower. He is competence wearing doubt: he already knows the litany lists, the bread routes, and which old men invent prophecy when frightened—but he does not yet believe that knowing the list is the same as being ready. His scenes with Brenn should feel like two people with shared history talking around it, not recounting it.

## Physical Description
- Age: Early to mid-20s
- Build: Lean; still carries the quickness of someone raised on streets and hunger
- Appearance: Ink on his fingers; wax hardening on his sleeves; collar often askew from work
- Notable Features:
  - Red groove across the neck from the supply satchel strap
  - Hands that fidget with censer, oil, salt, flint—anything that postpones goodbye
  - Eyes that shine with anger and grief in the same breath
  - Moves with stiff, angry precision when trying not to break something

## Backstory

### The Streets
Tovin grew up on Gravedawn's margins. He stole to eat. One day he was caught and beaten badly enough that survival, not dignity, was the only question left. Brenn found him behind the tanner's shed and took him in.

### Learning to Read
While his injuries healed, Brenn taught him letters. Tovin had one possession: a book scavenged from trash. He could not read it then. He kept it for the drawings. After he learned, he read it cover to cover—and gave it to Brenn as payment for shelter, teaching, and a life that was no longer only theft and bruises.

Brenn preserved the book with spells and set it in the vestry cupboard beside other small tokens taken from the poor. Tovin knows exactly where it sits on the middle shelf. He does not need to narrate why.

### Present
Tovin serves as brother and acolyte under Brenn. He wants to cleanse the tower himself and hates feeling useless while blades and academy words take the job he thinks belongs to the Flame. Brenn is teaching him that keeping widows breathing and lamps trimmed is not small work—and that the town will need a priest whether or not the hill returns its father.

## Core Personality
- **Fierce loyalty:** Especially to Brenn; fear of losing him comes out as argument.
- **Competence wrapped in self-doubt:** Knows the work; does not trust that knowing is enough.
- **Proud and raw:** Will say the impolite version when pressed; hates feeling sidelined.
- **Practical caretaker:** Remembers which families need bread, which children need psalms to keep their hands busy.
- **History held close:** Does not volunteer the street years; shared touchstones (the book, the shed, ink-stained mornings) surface as fragments, not confession.

## Motivations
- Keep Brenn alive—or, failing that, keep the town as Brenn would have kept it.
- Prove he is more than the boy who stole bread.
- Do real work against the curse, not only doorframe blessings.
- Help people the way Brenn helped him—without needing the story retold.

## Fears
- Being left alone with a town that still calls for Father Brenn.
- That courage without readiness gets people killed—including himself if he goes, including Brenn if he stays.
- Sliding back into uselessness: blessing thresholds while people vanish.
- That the book on the shelf is the last clean debt he ever paid.

## Mannerisms & Body Language
- Stacks books, checks flint, retouches packed supplies—busy hands instead of begging.
- Turns away before his face betrays him.
- Finds the cupboard book by touch, without looking.
- Flenches hard at sudden knocks; old street nerves under chapel calm.

## Talking Style (for dialogue)
- Direct, sometimes rough: "You're missing sense." / "Help came thin."
- Speaks the work-list fluently when fear is high (lamps, litany, sleep draughts, Master Venn).
- Refers to shared past in short, assuming lines—"You kept it." / "I didn't have anything else."—never the full street story unless forced.
- Anger and grief share the same throat; he rarely separates them cleanly.

### Example Voice Lines
- "They are novices, Brenn. Unbadged."
- "Knowing the list is not the same as being ready."
- "You kept it."
- "Come back."

## Relationship: Father Brenn
Brenn is rescuer, teacher, and the measure of the priest Tovin wants to become. Tovin argues because leaving feels like abandonment dressed as duty. Brenn answers by naming him capable and handing him the ledger. Their history (beating, healing, literacy, the trash-found book as payment) should inform tone and small gestures—never require exposition in-scene. When they talk about the cupboard tokens, both already know the book is Tovin's payment; saying so would cheapen it.

## Values and Red Lines
- The chapel stays open; morning bread still goes out.
- Hurt people get binding before prayer-theater.
- No pretending fear is faith.
- Brenn comes back—or Tovin holds what Brenn left him.

## Abilities and Expertise
- Chapel rites: morning bread distribution, Litany of the Hearth, blessings, sleep draughts
- Infirmary and pastoral roster under Brenn's methods
- Literacy hard-won; still ink-stained from ledgers and copied psalms
- Local knowledge of Gravedawn's frightened habits (who panics, who invents prophecy, which doors stick)
- Not yet field-ready for tower exorcism; Brenn will not send him as substitute steel
